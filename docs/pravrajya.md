# Pravrājya / Sannyāsa

Source: Varāhamihira, *Bṛhat Jātaka*, XV.1–4 and the accompanying notes,
N. Chidambaram Iyer's 1885 translation:
[chapter text](https://chestofbooks.com/new-age/astrology/Brihat-Jataka/Chapter-XV-On-Ascetio-Yogas.html),
[edition record](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/afmgm695).

The detector groups six positional clauses into one `Pravrajya Yoga` finding.

| Clause | Conditions |
| --- | --- |
| XV.1–2 | Four or more classical grahas in one sign, with at least one strong; strongest member determines the traditional order. |
| XV.3a | Moon's sign lord aspects Saturn and receives no other graha's aspect. |
| XV.3b | Strong Saturn aspects the weak Moon-sign lord. |
| XV.3c | Moon in Saturn's drekkana and Saturn's or Mars's navamsa, aspected only by Saturn. |
| XV.4a | Jupiter in natal ninth; Saturn aspects Jupiter, Moon and natal ascendant. |
| XV.4b | Saturn in natal ninth, unaspected by other grahas. |

For XV.1–2, combustion of the leading planet qualifies initiation as attachment
to renunciants. Planetary defeat qualifies it as a return to worldly life;
defeat with an additional aspect qualifies it as aspiration rather than initiation.
Tied leaders retain their individual qualifications without inventing a winner.
XV.4's additional author/ruler outcomes need a Raja-yoga assessment; the detector
reports the positional ascetic clauses following Iyer's note (b), not those outcomes.

Conventions are explicit: only D1 with the birth ascendant; seven grahas, excluding
nodes; full sign-based graha aspects, excluding conjunction; Parashari D3/D9;
current Shadbala ratio to each graha's required minimum for strength and ranking.
The last convention is the application's numerical interpretation of “powerful,”
not a threshold specified by the verse. All conjunction members need measured
strength before assigning the strongest. Missing strength is not weakness.
Planetary-war results use the existing Shadbala engine's convention.

Different translations/commentaries vary. This implements the named edition,
not a claim that all traditions agree. Successive initiations, dasha timing,
and a complete synthesis with Raja yogas are outside the detector.
The library passage is mixed rather than categorising renunciation as good or bad.
